# GOV.UK voice

> Unofficial. Distilled from the GOV.UK style guide and content design guidance,
> published by the UK Government Digital Service. Not endorsed by GDS.

## Voice in one line

Tell people what they need to do, in the fewest plain words, as if they're
reading on a phone while stressed.

## Tone rules

- Write in plain English. Assume your reader is busy and may not read English as a first language.
- Lead with what the reader needs to know or do. Put the most important words first in headings, sentences and links.
- Address the reader as "you". Refer to the organization as "we".
- Use the active voice. Say who does what.
- Be specific. Say what you're actually doing instead of using an abstract verb.
- Don't use metaphors. They slow people down and don't say what you mean.
- Use "cannot", "do not", "will not". Negative contractions are easy to misread as their opposite.
- Avoid complex contractions like "should've" and "would've". Simple positive ones like "you're" are fine.
- Don't use "please" to soften an instruction. Just give the instruction.
- Be neutral. No hype, no jokes, no exclamation marks.

## Banned phrases

GOV.UK publishes a list of words to avoid. Use the plain alternative.

- `agenda` → plan (unless it's for a meeting)
- `collaborate` → work with
- `deliver` → make, create, provide
- `empower` → allow, let
- `facilitate` → say what you're doing: run, help, pay for
- `foster` → encourage, help
- `impact` → affect, have an effect on
- `initiate` → start
- key, as an adjective ("a key priority") → important, or cut it. Not scored: a word matcher cannot tell it from "API key".
- `leverage` → use
- `liaise` → work with
- `overarching` → (cut it)
- `robust` → well thought out, thorough
- `streamline` → simplify
- `tackle` → stop, solve, deal with
- `transform` → say what changes
- `utilise` → use
- `utilize` → use
- `going forward` → from now on, in the future
- `moving forward` → from now on, in the future
- `in order to` → to
- `one-stop shop` → website, service
- `drive` → create, cause, encourage
- `please` → (cut it)
- `can't` → cannot
- `don't` → do not
- `won't` → will not

## Length

- Aim for 8 to 18 words per sentence on average.
- Check any sentence over 25 words. Split it if you can.
- Keep paragraphs to 1 to 3 sentences.
- Cut every word that doesn't help the reader do the task.

## Formatting

- Use sentence case for headings. No full stop at the end of a heading.
- Use bullets with a lead-in line. Start each bullet with a lowercase letter, one sentence per bullet, no semicolons, no full stop after the last bullet.
- Use numbered steps for a process the reader must follow in order.
- Write numbers as numerals, including 1 to 9, except "one" in phrases like "one of the".
- Write dates as "1 March 2027". Write times as "5:30pm". Use "to" in ranges: "10am to 2pm".
- Don't use "and/or". Don't use ampersands in body text.
- Make link text describe where the link goes. Never write "click here".

## Before and after

### Service update

**Before**

> We are pleased to announce that, going forward, applicants will be empowered to leverage our streamlined online portal in order to facilitate the submission of their documentation!

**After**

> You can now send your documents online. It takes about 10 minutes. You do not need to post anything.

### Error message

**Before**

> Unfortunately, your application can't be processed at this time as certain required fields haven't been completed. Please review your submission.

**After**

> Your application is not complete. Enter your date of birth and National Insurance number, then send it again.

### Eligibility

**Before**

> It is important to note that eligibility for this robust support scheme may be impacted by a number of key factors, which are outlined below for your convenience.

**After**

> You can get this support if you live in England and earn less than £20,000 a year. Check if you're eligible.

### Deadline

**Before**

> Payments should be submitted in a timely manner, and failure to comply with the deadline may result in penalties being applied to your account.

**After**

> Pay by 31 January. If you pay late, you'll get a £100 penalty.
