# Microsoft voice

> Unofficial. Distilled from the Microsoft Writing Style Guide, published on
> Microsoft Learn. Not endorsed by Microsoft.

## Voice in one line

Bigger ideas, fewer words: say the most useful thing first, like a friendly
person who's on the reader's side.

## Tone rules

- Be warm and relaxed. Write like you speak, then read it aloud to check.
- Be crisp and clear. Write for scanning first, reading second.
- Be ready to lend a hand. Anticipate what the reader needs next and make the next step obvious.
- Get to the point fast. Put the most important thing first and front-load keywords.
- Give people just enough information to decide with confidence. Prune every extra word.
- Start most statements with a verb.
- Cut "you can" when the sentence works without it.
- Avoid weak openings like "there is", "there are", and "there were".
- Use contractions: it's, you'll, you're, we're, let's.
- Use short, everyday words. Shun jargon and acronyms.
- Choose optimistic language. Short sentences and fragments are fine when they scan better.

## Banned phrases

- `there is` → (start with the subject or verb)
- `there are` → (start with the subject or verb)
- `there were` → (start with the subject or verb)
- `in order to` → to
- `utilize` → use
- `leverage` → use
- `purchase` → buy
- `please note` → (cut it)
- `it is recommended` → we recommend, or give the instruction
- `in the event that` → if
- `prior to` → before
- `is able to` → can

## Length

- Aim for 6 to 16 words per sentence on average. Shorter is better.
- Split any sentence over 25 words.
- Keep paragraphs to 1 to 3 sentences. Break content up, step it out, and layer details.

## Formatting

- Use sentence-style capitalization everywhere. When in doubt, don't capitalize.
- Don't end titles, headings, subheadings, or UI titles with a period or colon.
- Use the Oxford comma in lists of three or more.
- Use one space after periods, question marks, and colons.
- Use em dashes with no spaces around them.
- Use numbered lists for steps and bulleted lists for options.

## Terminology

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| buy | purchase |
| use | utilize, leverage |
| to | in order to |
| if | in the event that |
| before | prior to |
| sign in, sign out | log in, log on, log out |
| select | click (unless you mean a mouse action) |

## Before and after

### Call to action

**Before**

> If you are ready to purchase a subscription for your organization, it is recommended that you contact your account representative.

**After**

> Ready to buy? Contact us.

### Error message

**Before**

> Invalid ID.

**After**

> You need an ID that looks like this: someone@example.com

### Feature description

**Before**

> There are templates that can be utilized as a starting point for the creation of new documents. A template is able to include styles, formats, and page layouts.

**After**

> Save time with a template. Add the styles and layouts you use most, then start every new document from it.

### Settings

**Before**

> You can access your files across your devices, and there is also online storage and sharing that you are able to use.

**After**

> Store files online, open them on any device, and share them with coworkers.
