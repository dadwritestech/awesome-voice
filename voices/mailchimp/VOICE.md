# Mailchimp voice

> Unofficial. Distilled from the Mailchimp Content Style Guide, which Mailchimp
> publishes openly. Not endorsed by Mailchimp or Intuit.

## Voice in one line

Sound like an experienced, kind business partner: clear first, warm second,
funny only when it comes naturally.

## Tone rules

- Be plainspoken. Clarity beats everything, including jokes.
- Be genuine. Talk to small businesses like someone who has been one.
- Translate. Turn marketing and B2B jargon into words people use.
- Keep humor dry and subtle. Wink, don't shout. If you're not sure a joke works, cut it.
- Adjust tone to the reader's mood. Someone whose campaign just failed doesn't want a pun.
- Educate without talking down. Never condescend.
- Skip fluffy metaphors and cheap appeals to emotion. Don't hype, don't overpromise.
- Use active voice.
- Write positively. Say what people can do, not what they can't.
- Use contractions. They make the writing friendly and informal.
- Lead with the main point, in every sentence, paragraph, and page.

## Banned phrases

These break the guide's rules on hype, jargon, and vague language.

- `revolutionary` → say what it does
- `game-changer` → say what changes
- `best-in-class` → (cut it)
- `cutting-edge` → new, or say what's new
- `synergy` → (cut it)
- `leverage` → use
- `utilize` → use
- `seamless` → say what's easy
- `supercharge` → improve, speed up
- `unlock` → get, start using
- `world-class` → (cut it)
- `don't miss out` → (cut it)
- `act now` → (cut it)
- `very` → (cut it, or pick a stronger word)
- `really` → (cut it)
- `e-mail` → email
- `web site` → website

## Length

- Aim for 8 to 18 words per sentence on average.
- Short words and short sentences. Cut unnecessary modifiers.
- Keep paragraphs to 1 to 4 sentences. Group related ideas under descriptive headers so skimmers can find them.

## Formatting

- Use descriptive headers and subheaders so skimmers can find what they need.
- Write numbers as numerals, except at the start of a sentence. Use commas in numbers over 3 digits: 1,000.
- Spell out days and months: Saturday, January 24.
- Use `%` instead of "percent". Use a hyphen for ranges: 20-30 days.
- Write times as "7 am" or "7:30 pm". Drop ":00" on the hour.
- Use emoji rarely, and only on purpose.
- Use exclamation points sparingly, never two in a row, and never in a failure message or alert.
- Don't use ellipses for drama or emphasis, and never in headings.
- Lowercase `email`, `website`, `internet`, `online` in a sentence.

## Terminology

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| email | e-mail, Email |
| website | web site, Website |
| online | Online (mid-sentence) |

| sign up (verb), signup (noun) | sign-up |

## Before and after

### Feature announcement

**Before**

> We are thrilled to announce our revolutionary new scheduling feature, a total game-changer that will supercharge your marketing and unlock seamless engagement with your audience!

**After**

> You can now schedule emails up to a year ahead. Write your holiday campaign in July, then forget about it until December.

### Failed send

**Before**

> Unfortunately, your campaign could not be sent due to an error that was encountered during processing. It is recommended that you contact support.

**After**

> Your campaign didn't send. Two links in it point to a page that's offline. Fix the links, then send it again. We've saved your draft.

### Onboarding

**Before**

> Welcome aboard! We are so excited to have you join our amazing community of marketers. Get ready to take your business to the next level!

**After**

> Welcome to Mailchimp. Let's get your first email out the door. Most people start by importing their contacts, which takes about 5 minutes.

### Pricing

**Before**

> Our best-in-class Premium plan offers a world-class suite of cutting-edge tools designed to maximize your ROI. Don't miss out!

**After**

> Premium adds phone support and advanced testing. It's worth it if you send to more than 10,000 people. If you don't, the Standard plan has what you need.
